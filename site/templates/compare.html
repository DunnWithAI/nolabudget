How these figures work

  • What is counted. What each city government spent in its {{ year }} fiscal year on running each service and on construction and equipment for it. That covers salaries, benefits and other running costs.
  • Per resident. Each city's spending divided by the Census Bureau's population for that government. For New Orleans that is {{ nola.population|number }} people, which is higher than the estimate used elsewhere on this site, so these figures per resident run a little lower than the department pages.
  • Who does the work. Cities split services with other governments in different ways. A county, a sheriff or a separate district may also do some of the work, and its spending is not counted here. New Orleans's city and parish governments are one, as are {{ combined|rejectattr('is_nola')|map(attribute='name')|join(', ') }}. A city that reported no spending of its own on a service is left out of that comparison. A city that reports a little may share the service with another government, which is why this page compares with the median rather than the average.
  • Similar size. Every U.S. city government with 250,000 to 750,000 residents in the Census count. No city was picked by hand.
  • What's not here. Census also reports housing and courts, but its figures for New Orleans don't match the city's budget books in ways this site can explain, so they are left out. Water, sewer and the airport are run by separate boards that many cities don't have.
  • Why not other countries. Cities in other countries often don't run their own police, schools or health care, and they use different currencies and accounting rules. A comparison would mostly show what each city is responsible for, not how it spends.

The Census Bureau publishes these figures in the public domain. Get the data from the Census Bureau.
